DAY 3 · VISAS, MONEY AND WHAT YOU OWN - QUIZ
22 questions | 70% to pass | unlimited retries

Name: ______________________________     Date: ______________


1.  Who decides whether a visa is approved?

    1)  Atlys, based on the strength of the file
    2)  The consular officer reviewing the application
    3)  An automated eligibility check on the portal
    4)  The appointment centre (VFS) at the interview

2.  What is Atlys's role in the process?

    1)  Advocating for the applicant with the embassy
    2)  Guaranteeing the outcome for eligible applicants
    3)  Issuing the visa once documents are verified
    4)  Getting the file right and the appointment booked, not influencing the decision

3.  The doc says most refusals "aren't about the person, they're about the file." What does that imply for your job?

    1)  File quality matters less than which appointment slot you get
    2)  The avoidable failure (gaps in the file) is the thing you can actually sell against
    3)  You can tell strong applicants that they will be approved
    4)  Refusals are the applicant's fault and not worth discussing

4.  A guest asks you how long the appointment queue is for their city. What is the correct instinct?

    1)  Give the average from the last few applications
    2)  Avoid the question and move to the documents
    3)  Tell them what the slots actually show today, and be explicit that the queue is not something we control
    4)  Say it is usually fine at this time of year

5.  A guest on a route covered by AtlysProtect asks, on WhatsApp, exactly what they get back if the visa is refused. What do you put in writing?

    1)  The total they paid, including the government fee, since a refusal means they got nothing for it
    2)  The government fee, because the service fee has already paid for work we did
    3)  The service fee on any route, because the cover is standard on everything we file
    4)  The service fee comes back and the government fee does not, once you have confirmed this route is under AtlysProtect

6.  A guest was refused a US visa last year through another agent, paid for it, and is frightened of spending money again. What do you lead with?

    1)  That they can reapply straight away, since a refusal carries no waiting period
    2)  We decode why it was refused, build a personalised recovery plan and reapply for them, and they pay only when they are approved
    3)  That a previous refusal does not block a new application, so there is nothing to worry about this time
    4)  That the old refusal reason matters less than making the new file stronger than the last one

7.  A guest asks how long their visa will take. The country page gives a number and they were hoping for better. What do you commit to?

    1)  No number until the appointment is booked, because the queue is not ours to control
    2)  A slightly longer number than the page, so there is a buffer if the consulate is slow
    3)  What the last few applications on this route actually took, since that is closer to reality
    4)  The number on the country page, and a genuine special case gets revised with Ops rather than by you

8.  A guest starting a US B1/B2 asks which documents they need to send you. What is the correct answer?

    1)  Nothing yet. The list depends on the consulate and comes through once the appointment is booked
    2)  The standard set (passport scan, bank statement, ITR and photographs), and you send the checklist over
    3)  None. The only thing they have to fill is the DS-160, and it is worth checking whether they already have
    4)  A bank statement strong enough to show they can fund the trip, since that is what refusals turn on

9.  A guest asks what you actually do on the DS-160 that they could not do themselves. What is the honest answer?

    1)  Nothing beyond submitting it. The form is the applicant's own responsibility
    2)  Guide them through it, prefill what we can, and review every section against their passport and supporting documents before submission
    3)  File it on their behalf, so they never have to look at the form at all
    4)  Have it checked by our contact at the consulate before it is submitted

10.  A nervous guest asks what actually happens at the US interview. What do you tell them?

    1)  They should carry every document they own, because the officer works through the whole file
    2)  Almost everyone aged 14 to 79 attends in person; the officer asks about purpose, duration, funding, ties to India and travel history, and the answers must match the DS-160
    3)  We will prepare their answers together beforehand so the account comes out consistent and convincing
    4)  It is a formality. Most applicants are through in two minutes and it rarely changes anything

11.  A guest is travelling to France for five nights and Spain for three. Which embassy do they apply to?

    1)  Whichever of the two has appointment slots available soonest
    2)  The first country they enter, so it depends on the flight route
    3)  France, because that is where they spend the most nights
    4)  Either, since a Schengen visa is valid across the whole zone regardless of issuer

12.  What must a Schengen applicant's travel insurance cover?

    1)  There is no minimum; any policy naming the destination is accepted
    2)  At least €30,000, valid across the entire Schengen area for the full duration of the trip
    3)  At least €10,000, provided medical evacuation is included
    4)  At least €30,000, valid in the country whose embassy they applied to

13.  A guest with a 10-year US visa asks how long they are allowed to stay. What is the correct answer?

    1)  As long as the travel dates they entered on the DS-160
    2)  Six months in total, spread across the ten years
    3)  The visa lets them travel for 10 years; each entry is typically up to six months and the CBP officer at the port decides
    4)  Ten years of continuous stay

14.  A guest's US application has gone into 221(g) administrative processing. What do you tell them it means?

    1)  A request to complete the DS-160 again from scratch
    2)  It is not a refusal: additional processing after the interview, which needs careful follow-up
    3)  A refusal they can appeal within 30 days
    4)  A 12-month bar on reapplying

15.  A guest needs a UK standard visitor visa and travels in eight days. What do you say?

    1)  Super priority is next working day and priority is five, but check the biometrics appointment before committing to either
    2)  Apply on standard and request an upgrade later if it looks tight
    3)  Priority at five working days will definitely make it in time
    4)  Standard processing is three weeks, so they should postpone the trip

16.  What decides whether a guest needs an eTA or a Temporary Resident Visa for Canada?

    1)  Nationality: citizens of visa-exempt countries need an eTA, everyone else needs a TRV
    2)  Purpose of travel, with tourism on an eTA and business on a TRV
    3)  Whether they have visited Canada before
    4)  Trip length, with under six months qualifying for an eTA

17.  A guest holds an Egypt eVisa and plans to spend part of the trip in Sinai. What do you flag?

    1)  Sinai needs a separate permit; the eVisa does not cover it
    2)  Sinai requires a visa on arrival instead of an eVisa
    3)  Nothing, because the eVisa covers all of Egypt
    4)  They will need the multiple-entry version of the eVisa

18.  A guest wants to spend 45 days in Thailand. What do you recommend?

    1)  A visa on arrival, which allows 45 days
    2)  Travel on the 30-day exemption and extend locally once there
    3)  The 30-day exemption, with a short trip out of the country and back
    4)  The eVisa, which covers up to 60 days, because the 30-day exemption does not cover the trip and overstay penalties are severe

19.  What makes a lead hot rather than cold?

    1)  The size of the order
    2)  Whether they arrived by chat or by call
    3)  How many messages they have sent
    4)  Intent and urgency

20.  A conversation needs to pass to someone else mid-way. What matters most?

    1)  Waiting until the guest asks to speak to someone else
    2)  Making sure the guest knows it was not your fault
    3)  Closing the chat quickly to protect your metrics
    4)  A clean handover. The next person has the context and the guest is not asked to repeat themselves

21.  A consult ends without payment. The guest is interested but wants to think. What happens next?

    1)  Leave them room to decide and check back in a few days so it does not feel like pressure
    2)  A structured WhatsApp follow-up within two minutes, then a defined three-touch cadence
    3)  Send the pricing page again so they have everything in writing to compare
    4)  Mark the lead cold and move to the next one. They did not close on the call

22.  A guest calls from the airport. They have been denied check-in over a visa problem. What do you do?

    1)  Refer them to the airline, since check-in is the airline's decision
    2)  Escalate to the Emergency Helpline immediately, stay on the line until handover is confirmed, and never hold them longer than 60 seconds
    3)  Take the details and commit to calling back within two hours
    4)  Put them on hold, check the case in Cadence, then advise them directly
