01   ASHUTOSH, THE HONEST VERSION
 
It is a Tuesday in March, nine at night. A Bandra skincare brand has sent you 40 minutes of phone footage and a Thursday deadline. You cut it in DaVinci Resolve, drop a LUT you built yourself, pull a track from Epidemic Sound, and send a Frame.io link before midnight. They approve with two comments. That evening covers your rent for the week.
The gap right now is not software. It is that you have never finished anything on somebody else's deadline. Beginners quit around week five, when the timeline stops being fun and turns into admin: renaming clips, chasing feedback, re-exporting at 2am because the first render had the wrong audio track. Three unfinished projects, zero delivered. Drafts folder mein sannata hai.
The finishers publish one imperfect cut on a fixed day every week. The stallers keep polishing a single project nobody has seen. Shipping on a schedule teaches you more in one month than perfecting one edit teaches in six.
02   WHERE YOU STAND TODAY
 
YOUR LEVEL18%
 
Eighteen percent means you can sit on a timeline and export something watchable. What is missing is everything around the cut: pacing that holds a viewer past eight seconds, audio that never clips, colour that matches between two shots, and a file structure you can hand to a client without explaining it. At 90 minutes a day, first paid work is about 14 weeks away.
03   THE FOUR STAGES
 
01
BeginnerWeek 1 to 6
One editor, learned properly. The free DaVinci Resolve 19. Keyboard before effects: J K L, blade, ripple delete, three point edit. Cut footage you did not shoot, from Pexels, so you are judging pacing and not your own memories. Export one 60 second piece every day.
→  You can turn somebody else's raw footage into a clean watchable minute, on request.
02
IntermediateWeek 7 to 20
Audio and colour, the two things amateurs skip. Clean dialogue with Voice Isolation, mix to minus 14 LUFS on the Fairlight page, retire the stock whoosh. Node based grading and shot matching on scopes. One motion tool, Fusion or After Effects. Two free edits for local brands.
→  Small brands pay you for reels, because yours hold attention past the third second.
03
AdvancedMonth 6 to 9
Structure beats technique. Study how Dhruv Rathee stacks an explainer and how Peter McKinnon builds tension inside 90 seconds. Build your own template, LUT pack and proxy workflow so a 20 minute podcast turns around in four hours. Take revisions without rebuilding the timeline.
→  You quote by the project instead of the hour, and clients stop negotiating it.
04
MasterMonth 9 to 12
Now you are hired for judgement. Directing the shoot so the edit is easy, running two junior editors through Frame.io, owning a channel's retention graph rather than its cuts. Retainers replace one off jobs. You refuse footage that cannot be saved, which is itself a paid skill.
→  Retainers cover your month before it starts and referrals do all of your selling.

05   YOUR FIRST 30 DAYS
 
01
Week 121 Sep to 27 Sep
Install Resolve 19 and touch the timeline only with the keyboard for seven days. Pull a free Pexels clip pack and build one 60 second cut daily to a fixed song. You know it is right when every cut lands on a beat without you nudging it.
→  Seven daily 60 second cuts
02
Week 228 Sep to 4 Oct
Audio week. Record 30 seconds of your own voice daily, clean it with Voice Isolation, mix to minus 14 LUFS on the Fairlight page. You know it is right when the voice sits above the music and nobody reaches for the volume.
→  One clean narrated edit
03
Week 35 Oct to 11 Oct
Colour week. Shoot two phone clips in different light, then match them on the Color page using the parade scope rather than your eyes. Build one LUT you will reuse. You know it is right when the cut between the two shots goes unnoticed.
→  Your first reusable LUT
04
Week 412 Oct to 18 Oct
Deadline week. Ask one cafe or gym near you for footage they already have and deliver a 30 second reel inside 48 hours, free. Send it on Frame.io, take their notes without arguing, send version two. You know it is right when they post it.
→  A published client reel
